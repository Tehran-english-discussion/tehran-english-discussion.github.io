Cozy videos, soft lives, “good vibes only.” Beyond the trendy word, a real question: how do we define a good life today, and what actually nourishes us?

Why this topic

We’re going to talk about wholesome, and beyond being just a trendy word, it pushes us to examine how we define a good life today.

In a world hooked on speed, drama, and constant performance, this topic challenges us to ask whether genuine peace, warmth, and emotional safety can coexist with ambition, honesty, and personal growth, or if chasing “wholesomeness” sometimes turns into avoiding hard truths and real friction.

Ultimately, it’s a conversation about the fine line between what truly nourishes us and what merely looks sweet on the surface.

    The fine line

    Can they live together?

    Maede’s question, split three ways. For each pair: allies, enemies, or something in between?

    Soft

    Peace

    Slow mornings, no drama, protecting your calm.

    &?
    Sharp

    Ambition

    Big goals, long hours, wanting more than you have.

    Is “protecting my peace” sometimes just a nicer way of saying “I’ve stopped trying”?

    Soft

    Warmth

    Kindness, encouragement, a group where everyone feels welcome.

    &?
    Sharp

    Honesty

    Saying the thing nobody wants to hear, including to a friend.

    Can a friendship be truly warm if nobody in it ever tells you a hard truth?

    Soft

    Safety

    Feeling accepted as you are, with nothing to prove.

    &?
    Sharp

    Growth

    Friction, discomfort, being challenged to become someone new.

    Do we grow because we feel safe, or because something made us uncomfortable?

    At the table

    Questions for Friday

    Think about these before you come. We’ll talk them through together.

    1. What does “wholesome” mean to you? Has the word changed meaning in your lifetime?
    2. Why do you think wholesome and cozy content became so popular right now, in a world of speed, drama and constant performance?
    3. Think of the most wholesome person you know. Are they also honest with you when it matters?
    4. Can a life be peaceful and ambitious at the same time? Have you seen anyone pull it off?
    5. When does “keeping things nice” turn into avoiding a conversation that needs to happen?
    6. Is some friction necessary for a relationship, a team or a group like ours to stay healthy?
    7. What’s something in your life that looks sweet on the surface but doesn’t really nourish you? And the opposite: something hard that does?
    8. How would you define a good life today, in one sentence? Would your parents’ generation agree?
    The fine line

    Bread or cupcake? Most good lives need a bit of both. The trick is knowing which one you’re reaching for, and why.
