An old Greek myth

The golden touch

There is an old Greek myth where a king was once offered to make any wish he wanted. Midas (the king) asked that whatever he might touch should be changed into gold. So Dionysus granted the man’s wish. However, there was a catch. With the cunning old Greek gods there was always a catch.

At first, Midas touched flowers, trees, and stones; they all turned into gold. He felt happy. He finally had never-ending wealth. He returned to his palace and had the servants prepare a large feast so they could celebrate this newfound power.

He picked up a cup of wine, and the cup and the wine turned into solid gold. Whatever he tried to eat turned into gold.

That was when he realized that this golden touch was no blessing. Rather, it was a curse.

P.S.The picture shows another version of the story, where Midas had turned the roses in the garden into gold and his daughter was sad about the lost flowers. He tried to comfort his daughter and accidentally turned her into a golden statue.

At the table

Sample questions

Think about these before Friday. We’ll talk about them together at the table.

  1. Would you rather have more money and less free time, or less money and more free time? Has your answer changed over the years?
  2. Is there anything you willingly pay extra for because it saves you time or effort?
  3. What’s something people desperately want but often underestimate the cost of achieving?
  4. Would you accept a highly successful career if it meant sacrificing most of your personal time? How much would be too much?
  5. What’s something that sounded like a dream come true until you actually achieved it or lived it, and realized its huge downsides?
  6. Is making compromises an essential part of a happy life, or should we refuse to settle for less than what we really want?
