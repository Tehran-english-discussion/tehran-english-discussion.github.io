The discussion

Four lenses on beauty

Sample questions for the table. Come with your opinions, your examples and your curiosity. There are no wrong answers, only interesting ones.

The brain

Psychology and cognitive science: what happens inside us when we call something beautiful?

  1. When you see something beautiful, is it a feeling, a judgement, or both? Can you describe the moment it happens?
  2. In experiments, babies only a few months old look longer at faces that adults rate as attractive. If that’s true, what does it tell us?
  3. Sunsets, the sea, a starry sky: is there anything that everyone finds beautiful?
  4. Can something be beautiful and useless? Beautiful and dangerous?

Universal or cultural?

Are we born with a sense of beauty, or does the place we grow up in shape it?

  1. Think of something your grandparents found beautiful that you don’t, or the other way round.
  2. For centuries pale skin was a sign of wealth, because poor people worked in the sun. Later a tan became the sign of a rich holiday. What other beauty “rules” are really about money or status?
  3. Is Persian calligraphy beautiful to someone who can’t read it? Is music in a scale you’ve never heard beautiful?
  4. Can you find beauty in imperfection: a cracked wall, an old face, a worn-out book?

Media & mirrors

Screens, filters, brands and algorithms: who is holding up the mirror today?

  1. How have social media filters changed what a “normal” face looks like?
  2. Have you ever started liking a song, a style or a face just because you saw it everywhere? Psychologists call this the mere-exposure effect: the more we see something, the more we tend to like it.
  3. Who decides what is beautiful today: artists, brands, algorithms, or us?
  4. Should edited photos of faces and bodies in adverts carry a label?

Changing with time

Yesterday’s “ugly” is often today’s masterpiece. Why do our ideas of beauty keep moving?

  1. Name something that was called ugly when it first appeared and is loved now. Why did people change their minds?
  2. Which of today’s beauty standards will look strange in fifty years?
  3. Does beauty matter more, or less, as we get older?
  4. Is beauty something we discover, something we learn, or something society teaches us?
1874 · PARIS

“Impressionism”

A critic mocked Monet’s Impression, Sunrise as an unfinished sketch. His insult became the name of one of the most loved movements in art.

1887 · PARIS

The Eiffel Tower

Before it was built, a group of famous artists and writers signed a protest calling it “useless and monstrous.” Today it is the symbol of the city.

1920s · RIVIERA

The suntan

A tan once meant outdoor work. Fashion icons like Coco Chanel are often credited with turning it into a sign of leisure and style.

Vocabulary

Words worth bringing

A few words and phrases that will help on Friday.

noun · adjectiveaestheticA set of ideas about what looks good; also, relating to beauty.The café has a warm, vintage aesthetic.
phrasebeauty standardThe look a society holds up as ideal at a certain time.Beauty standards change every few decades.
phraseconventionally attractiveAttractive in the way most people in a culture expect.She isn’t conventionally attractive, but she’s unforgettable.
adjectivestrikingBeautiful in an unusual way that catches your attention.He has striking green eyes.
adjectiveexquisiteExtremely beautiful and delicate, often finely made.The tilework in the mosque is exquisite.
adjectivetacky · kitschCheap-looking or showy, in poor taste. Kitsch is sometimes enjoyed on purpose, for fun.Plastic flamingos are so kitsch I love them.
nounthe sublimeBeauty mixed with awe, even a little fear: a storm, a mountain, the night sky.Standing at the edge of Damavand felt sublime.
noun · Japanesewabi-sabiFinding beauty in things that are imperfect, simple and not permanent.A chipped old teacup has its own wabi-sabi.
nounsymmetry · proportionSymmetry: two halves that match. Proportion: how the sizes of parts relate to each other.Persian architecture loves symmetry.
idiombeauty is in the eye of the beholderWhat is beautiful depends on who is looking.You hate it, I love it. Beauty is in the eye of the beholder.
idiombeauty is only skin-deepLooks are on the surface; character matters more.Don’t judge him yet. Beauty is only skin-deep.
psychologymere-exposure effectWe tend to like things more simply because we have seen them often.That song grew on me. Mere exposure, I guess.
