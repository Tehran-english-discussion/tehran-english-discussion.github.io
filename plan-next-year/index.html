How to plan next year in times of war.

We can’t predict next month, let alone next year. So on Friday we learn five simple rules for planning in times of war, and talk through each one together. At home, you turn them into your own plan.

Before Friday

A note from the moderator

Hi everyone,

As you already know, our country is at war, and we are living in times of extreme uncertainty. The economy has been getting worse year by year, and most of us are in our 20s or 30s, the very years when we have the energy to build our lives and careers.

We could become victims and blame everything on the environment, which is not wrong, by the way. Or we could choose to take action and, even in the worst of times, build a fulfilling life for ourselves.

Some people think the only solution is to migrate to a better country, which is also a valid argument. But even as Iranians abroad, we still can’t make international transactions, and our currency has a low value, which makes the transition hard in the beginning. Being far away from our family, friends, and culture is hard, too. So migration is not a panacea. It might solve some problems, but it can create others, and it has its own hardships.

I believe each of us has a life purpose. No matter how messy our environment gets, we need to remember what that purpose is, put one foot in front of the other, and keep walking towards it.

I don’t have a one-size-fits-all answer to this question myself. I want to hear everyone’s thoughts and strategies, so that we can all make a better plan for the year ahead.

PouriaModerator

On Friday · at the table

5 rules for planning in times of war

One conversation, all of us together. We’ll go through the rules one by one, and each rule has one question to talk about. Think about them before Friday.

Celebrate each other’s ideasYou can always passAbout 1–2 minutes each
1

Fill your cup first

Big plans run on energy. The small things that make your days better, like good routines, good people, and good habits, come before any goal.

  1. At the table · go-around

    What’s one small thing that has been making your days better lately?

    Everyone, 30 seconds each.

2

Aim at what you control

Some things are out of our hands. Your skills, your habits, and your English are always yours to grow.

  1. At the table

    What’s one thing you’d love to be proud of by this time next year?

    A skill, a habit, or a project. Write it down for 3 minutes, then a few volunteers share.

3

Picture the win

Imagine the year has already gone well, and look back. The story shows you the path, and the bumps along the way.

  1. At the table

    It’s a year from now and you made it. Tell us the story!

    Follow-up: what almost stopped you, and how did you get past it?

4

Grow what goes with you

Life can take us anywhere. Skills, friendships, and good habits come with you wherever you go.

  1. At the table

    Which skill would make you stronger wherever life takes you?

5

Small steps, together

Big years are built from small steps, and steps are easier with friends cheering you on. Start small, start this week.

  1. At the table · go-around

    What’s your first small step this week, and who here will cheer you on?

    Everyone, 30 seconds each. In three months, we’ll get together to celebrate our wins.

Your notes from Friday

Write down the ideas you love from Friday, and the people who’ll cheer you on.

At home · before or after Friday

Plan your year, step by step

The same 5 rules, on paper. Eight questions, about 15 minutes. Imagine the year has gone well, spot what might get in the way, and take one small step.

Your notes stay on this device only. Nobody else can see them.

1

Look ahead

  1. What matters

    What matters most to you, and what are three things you hope to achieve in the next five years?

  2. What is one target you control that you want to hit by this time next year?

2

Spot the bumps

  1. What might get in the way, and how will you handle it?

3

Imagine it’s a year from now, and you did reach your target.

Now look back from the good ending. What got you there?

  1. What did you do, and what did you stop doing, that got you there?

  2. Which skills, people, and habits did you build along the way?

  3. Where did you refuse to give up, even when it was hard?

4

Stay flexible

  1. If life changes direction, like a move or a new job, how would your plan bend?

5

Start

  1. What is one small step you can take this week, who will you tell, and when will you check this plan again?
